## The one idea (intro)

> **The tools changed dramatically. The fundamentals didn't.**

- Code is cheap now. AI really does automate the prototype stage.
- "You can automate all of software development" is **not true** — and the people saying it are usually selling something.
- Judgment is the scarce resource: *what* to build, *for whom*, *whether the foundation holds*.
- Technical debt compounds faster than ever: more volume, less understanding.

---

## Prompt bank

Pull one when the room goes quiet. Each has a story attached.

### Technical roadblocks
- "What's the messiest part of your codebase? What if it's still that way in three months?"
- **Story:** we spent a lot of time rewriting a bad MVP.
- **Point:** fast ≠ careless. Product shortcuts (cut scope) are fine; engineering shortcuts (coupling, bad abstractions) are expensive. Know which decisions are hard to reverse.

### Design decisions
- "What's an early decision you'd make differently now? Is it expensive to change?"
- **Story:** ChatGPT launched mid-build and forced a pivot. The idea wasn't wrong — the best solution moved.
- **Point:** the skill is knowing what to preserve and what to abandon. *"If a model release killed half your implementation tomorrow, what would still be valuable?"*

### Idea → real product
- "Who is this for? What do they *already* spend time or money on?"
- **Story:** we chased leads from customer data and consultant advice. They didn't materialize.
- **Point:** the say/do/pay gap is the hardest problem in business, and AI hasn't touched it. Data tells you *that* something happened, not *why*. Consultants don't bear your consequences.
- Also: **right idea ≠ right business.** We had the right idea; still do.

### AI hype (raise it if nobody asks)
- Most "automate everything" content is grift.
- The trap: shipping careless code because "AI will clean it up later."
- AI is a good copilot and a bad architect. The judgment layer is still yours.

## Close

> You can make decisions that seem perfectly reasonable with the information available — and still be wrong. The skill is knowing what to keep and what to let go of.

| Preserve | Change |
| --- | --- |
| The problem you're solving | Your proposed solution |
| Standards for reliable systems | Your original architecture |
| Long-term vision | Immediate business strategy |
| Commitment to understanding customers | Your assumptions about their needs |
| Willingness to use evidence | Your interpretation of that evidence |
